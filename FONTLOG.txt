FONTLOG for odv Grotesk
=======================

odv Grotesk is Schibsted Grotesk with Greek and mathematical characters added,
for typesetting technical writing (LuaLaTeX) and for the odv.hu website.

Schibsted Grotesk: https://github.com/schibsted/schibsted-grotesk
Copyright 2023 The Schibsted-Grotesk Project Authors, SIL Open Font License 1.1.
odv Grotesk is a modified version under the same licence; it is not affiliated
with or endorsed by Schibsted.

ChangeLog
---------

0.3.7 (2026-11-22)
- C G S J 2 3 5 6 9: every curved terminal is cut level at the height of
  its inner corner, as in e c s, instead of on a slant of 11-17 degrees;
  the outer edge runs on along its own direction to the cut. The tabular
  figures and numerators follow, and with them the superiors and
  fractions (² ³ ½ ¾), and the accented capitals (Ç Ĝ Š...).
- Level-cut terminals (c e s and C G S J 2 3 5 6 9) no longer swell. The
  curved strokes grew heavier towards their ends (S: 148 units at the top
  of the bowl, 182-190 on the side, 216-220 at the ends, Regular), which
  the level cut showed as a heavy flat end. From the top or bottom of the
  bowl the outer edge now eases in, without a point where it starts, so
  the end is 0.45 of the way from the thin to the side of o (lowercase), O
  (capitals) or 0 (figures, numerators against the numerator 0): Regular
  160, 169 and 165 units. The inner edge and the cut are unchanged; ς,
  whose top is the c's, follows.
- r: the arm ends in a vertical cut, like the flags of f j y t, instead of
  one about 8 degrees off vertical (in the italics it follows the slant).
  The cut stands at the tip, and the underside of the arm runs on along
  its own curve to meet it, so the arm keeps its reach and the r its
  spacing. ŕ ŗ follow.
- Corners: where a straight edge turns through about a quarter into
  another by way of a curve, the curve's curvature jumped from nothing to
  its full value where it met the straight. These curves are redrawn as
  fair corners (scripts/fair.py, as in the a's bowl since 0.3.5): the
  curvature rises from nothing and falls back to nothing, so there is no
  point where the bend starts. Each starts where the old curve did. This
  touches the hooks of f j t y l and ȷ, g's ear, a's spur, ª, α μ π,
  { } ƒ ∫ √ ℓ @ Q and the drawn ζ ξ ς τ, in every style.
- s: 3% wider about its centre, its verticals thinned back so the stroke
  weights stay, the sidebearings kept (Regular 26 units wider). Cut level
  at both ends it read small beside z. ś š ş ș follow.
- Č č Ě ě ř Š š Ť Ž ž ǎ Ǧ are drawn by Schibsted as one outline each, so
  they did not follow what odv changes in their letters (level cuts, the
  a's top, the bars of E T Z z, the r's arm). They are rebuilt as the
  letter and the caron mark, which their carons are to a unit.
- Shoulders in Medium, Bold and their italics: where an arch or bowl meets
  a straight stem (n h m u r, b d p q), the contrast thinning moved the
  arch's outer edge in all the way to the stem, so the junction came out
  about half the weight of the arch's top. The arch is now thinned less
  and less towards the stem, easing from all of it at the top of the arch
  to nothing at the crotch, without a step in direction or curvature.
  The junction keeps Schibsted's weight and the notch about his depth.
- Crotches in Medium, Bold and their italics: where a curve meets another
  stroke (b d p q n h m u r a, 6 9 & $ ð æ), the contrast thinning moved
  the two sides apart and joined them with a short flat (46 units wide in
  Bold). The two sides are now carried on along their own course until
  they meet, so the crotch is a point again, as Schibsted drew it, a
  little deeper than his. Where two curves meet too sharply to be carried
  on (the foot of a in Bold, ª, @, ③), they are carried on in straight
  lines along their own direction, as long as the point lands within
  twice the old flat's width; the ð in Bold keeps its flat.
- Spacing: the bowls of b, p, þ (and ð β ρ, kerned with them) and of d, q
  sat 10-20 units closer to their neighbours than o, and the shoulder of h
  about 28 closer than n. Schibsted made up for it in the kerning, so the
  pairs it did not kern, or that 0.3.6 unkerned, sat tight: od, bd, pd, ad,
  hu. The difference now lives in the sidebearings, by the median of what
  Schibsted kerned each letter further than o or n (Regular: d q 18, b 14,
  þ 18, h 28 units; per style), and comes off the kerning again, so the
  pairs Schibsted kerned look as before. d, q: ď đ follow; h: ĥ ħ.
- 1: 20 units (of 2048) tighter on the left in every style, so the white
  under its flag no longer reads as a gap (1911, 1951). Pairs kerned
  closer to fit the flag, as after 5, keep the flag where it was.
- Kerning: no letter is kerned further from a round than from a stem. Pairs
  meeting a round side (o b p e c s before o d c e q a s; O D Q C G S B P
  before O C G Q S; the figures among themselves) are brought down to what
  the first letter gets against n or H, or to nothing: e-o, s-a, O-O, B-O,
  0-0, 9-6, 1-9 and the like were opened by 10-60 units. B G S, 3 and 8
  keep what they have against H.
- En dash (and em dash) between figures: every figure-dash pair opens by
  the same amount, so the dash keeps from 0 the distance one 0 keeps from
  the next (Regular 36-38 units, Medium 25-28, Italic 10-20, Medium
  Italic 4-13; Bold within 3, Bold Italic 7-14 closer). Year ranges such as 1967-2026 are no longer crowded
  round the dash. Dates are set in the default figures; tabular figures
  are for columns.

0.3.6 (2026-11-14)
- s: both terminals are cut level at the height of their inner corners, as
  in e and c, instead of on a slant; the outer edge runs on along its own
  direction to the cut. The spine is unchanged.
- a (upright styles): the terminal at the top of the arch is cut level at
  the height of its tip, instead of on a slant; the wedge under the tip is
  taken off, so the inner edge keeps its curve.
- Kerning: Schibsted kerned round lowercase pairs apart (o b p before
  o d c e q by 40-70 units), so pod and boo sat looser than no and much
  looser than oz. These pairs are now unkerned: two rounds sit a little
  closer than a round and a stem, as the sidebearings alone give. The
  kerning that pulls z towards o c e s is halved with it, so poz is even
  and ezzel holds together. The Greek letters in the same classes follow.
- The square dot overhangs the stem of i by the same amount on each side in
  every weight (12/2048 em): unchanged in Regular and Medium to a unit or
  two, 3% larger in Bold (276 units against 268). The full stop, comma,
  quotes and dieresis follow.

0.3.5 (2026-11-13)
- a (issue #19): the corner where the flat top of the bowl turns down is
  rounder and no longer has a point where the bend starts. Its curvature
  eases in from nothing on the flat, rises and falls evenly, and runs on
  into the bowl's own curvature without a step (scripts/fair.py: the corner
  is defined by its curvature, solved to land on the flat and the bowl). The
  outside of the bowl reaches a little further along the top than the
  counter, so the two flats begin together.
- Contrast thinning (Medium, Bold and their italics) keeps its weights but
  no longer leaves marks. Every stroke still loses the same amount on each
  side, but at the top and bottom of every round, where the two halves met
  in a point (o, e, s, 0, ©, the circled figures…), and at the top and
  bottom of counters, where they left a flat, both halves now run into a
  short curve that matches their direction and curvature. Terminal cuts
  keep their angle (the curve is cut where the cut moved to), crotches are
  cut where the two curves cross, and the inside corner of v keeps its
  short flat. The weights of stems, rounds, bars and diagonals are as
  before, to the unit.
- Schibsted's own small kinks are straightened (smooth points turning 0.5
  to 10 degrees, as in the § and some figures), and steps in curvature
  between two quadratic pieces, like the one at the bottom of g's lower
  counter, are evened out by sliding the point along its tangent, in every
  glyph of every style.
- Smooth points stay smooth in the TrueType files: they are rounded to the
  whole-unit position that keeps them smoothest, and gentle curves are no
  longer flattened into lines. Bold had about 250 visible kinks in 0.3.4,
  almost all from these and the thinning; 17 are left, the short flats at
  inside corners where strokes cross ($ ¢ ¤ & ð).
- . ! ? ¡ ¿ have square dots like the dot of i, the same size, on the
  baseline; : ; … and the middle dot ·, ŀ Ŀ and the tabular forms are made of
  the full stop and follow, the upper dot of the colon topping out at the
  x-height.
- , has a square head of the same size and a straight tail of even width
  leaning left, cut level, smaller than the round comma it replaces. ; ’ ”
  ‚ „ ʼ ŉ are made of it and follow; ‘ and “ are the same comma turned
  round. These and the full stop are not thinned in Medium and Bold, so the
  heads stay square.
- z E F T Z L H: in Medium and Bold the contrast thinning narrows the stems
  but not the bars, so the straight bars came out as heavy as the stems
  (Bold: 0.99 of the stem). They are back to Schibsted's proportion, about
  0.85: a bar on the baseline or the cap height keeps its outer edge, a
  middle bar thins about its centre, and Z's diagonal keeps its angle.
- i and j: stems 4.5% heavier than n's (87:85 looked thin next to n and l).

0.3.4 (2026-11-13)
- Accented Greek (issue #15). The monotonic letters ά έ ή ί ό ύ ώ ϊ ϋ ΐ ΰ,
  Ά Έ Ή Ί Ό Ύ Ώ Ϊ Ϋ and the spacing ΄ ΅ are added, built from the letter and
  the font's own acute and dieresis, so typed Greek (λέξη, δέλτα) no longer
  shows the accent beside the letter. On the capitals the tonos stands to the
  left of the letter; in ΐ ΰ the dots are spread so the acute stands between
  them, and the glyph is widened to hold them. The accented letters are
  kerned like their bases (the capitals not on the tonos side).
- Every Greek letter now has a top anchor, so combining accents typed after
  it (ζ́, ξ̈, decomposed input) sit over it, and after a Greek capital the
  accents take their flatter capital forms, as they do after A-Z.
- Added odv Math (issue #16): New Computer Modern Math Book 8.1.1 by Antonis
  Tsolomitis, enlarged to 112 %, the size at which odv-latex sets it next to
  odv Grotesk, so Keynote and the web get it at that size too. Only
  unitsPerEm changes (1000 -> 893); outlines, metrics and the MATH table are
  New Computer Modern's. Renamed, as the GUST Font License asks; it stays
  under that licence, with GUST-FONT-LICENSE.txt and README-odvMath.txt in
  the release. Built by scripts/odvmath.py from the pinned upstream file.

0.3.3 (2026-11-13)
- ν (nu) is no longer a copy of v: its left stroke drops straight from the
  x-height and turns through a round bottom, like υ's, into the foot of a
  steeper right arm, so ν = 0.3 (Poisson's ratio) can't be read as v = 0.3.
  Spaced like n on the left and like v on the right, in every style.
- τ (tau) no longer reads as ι: its crossbar is a tenth wider than t's and
  symmetric about the stem, and the foot stops halfway out to the end of the
  bar instead of right under it, so the bar clearly overhangs on both sides.
  Spaced like t.
- Italics: the drawn Greek and maths glyphs were spaced as if the italic
  were slanted about the baseline, but Schibsted's is slanted about
  mid-x-height, so their right sidebearings came out about 230 units too
  tight (τ ran into the next letter) and ∃ was shifted right. Φ Ψ γ δ ε ζ θ
  λ σ τ φ χ ψ ω ϕ ϵ ∈ ∃ are now spaced like their upright forms. The upright
  styles are unchanged.
- σ: the end of its bar is spaced like the end of t's bar (it had about half
  that), so it no longer runs into the bar of a following τ; στ are 20-30
  units further apart in every style. The bar is shorter by the same amount,
  so the bowl is as far from the next letter as before.
- Italic λ keeps 60% of the slant. Slanted in full, its long stroke stood
  almost upright and the right leg reached a third as far as in the upright
  styles. The feet and the spacing are where they were; the top sits about
  125 units further left.
- Kerning for the Greek letters (issue #4). Only punctuation was kerned
  against Schibsted's eight (Δ Π Σ Ω α β μ π) and nothing against the added
  ones, so ΓΑΤΑ and ΤΥΠΟΣ had wide gaps where AVATAR sits tight. Each Greek
  letter now takes each side's kerning from the Latin letter with the same
  side: Α-Χ from their Latin twins, Γ from E on the left and T on the right,
  Λ and Δ from A, Θ Φ Ω θ from O, Π from H, ο φ from o, and so on (the
  table is in scripts/kern.py). δ ζ λ, whose strokes rise to the ascender,
  take b's light kerning on the left so they don't run into T's arm, and
  ζ's right side is left unkerned. Schibsted's
  own pairs for its eight Greek letters (with quotes, figures, dashes, and
  among themselves in Bold) are kept as they were. Latin kerning is
  unchanged.
- i and j (issue #5): 0.3.1 made i's stem 6% heavier than n's but left j,
  l and the f of fi alone, so ij, illi and the fi ligature mixed two stem
  weights (Bold: 270 against 252). The i is now heavier by Akkurat's ratio
  instead (87:85, about 2.4%), measured in the finished font, after the
  contrast thinning, and j's stem matches it: 172 against l's 168 in Regular,
  258 against 252 in Bold. ĵ, ij, ι and the fi ligatures follow.
- Added ξ and ς (issue #7). ξ has ζ's tail and two bowls open to the right,
  the smaller upper one hung from the top bar; ς is the top of c, terminal
  cut level, running on along the baseline into the same tail.
- δ: the stroke above the bowl now curls over to the right and ends in a
  terminal cut level like c's, instead of running out flat at the ascender.
- Added ∉ ∩ ∪ ⊂ ⊃ ⊆ ⊇ ∧ ∨ ⇒ ⇔ ∝ ∥ ∠ △. ⊂ ⊃ ∪ ∩ are the curve of ∈ in its
  weight and size; ⊆ ⊇ add a bar as far below as the bars of = are apart;
  ∉ takes the slash of ≠, steeper. ∧ ∨ ∠ △ are monoline in the weight of +.
  ⇒ ⇔ keep the heads of → ↔ with the shaft doubled at the spacing of =; ∝
  is ∞ cut open; ∥ is two bars of |, as far apart as the bars of =.

0.3.2 (2026-11-12)
- Font files: head.fontRevision is now 0.302 (it dropped the patch number,
  so 0.3.0 and 0.3.1 both said 0.3). Manufacturer is o d v, the designer
  field credits both Schibsted Grotesk's designers and odv Grotesk, the
  vendor ID is no longer Bakken & Baeck's, and the copyright line no longer
  lists individual letters.
- Hinting removed from every style. Regular and Italic kept Schibsted's
  hints on the untouched glyphs only, so hinted and unhinted letters could
  render differently side by side on Windows.

0.3.1 (2026-10-22)
- i: the stem 6% heavier than n's, so it no longer reads thin next to the
  round letters; i, í, ì, î, ï, ij and the fi ligatures follow.
- p: the counter moved right until the stem and the bowl are equally thick at
  mid-height, which also widens the join of bowl and stem.

0.3.0 (2026-10-22)
- Contrast: in Medium, Bold and their italics every outline is thinned
  horizontally (verticals lose 17-46 units, horizontals unchanged), bringing
  the thin/stem ratio to 0.87. TrueType hinting is removed from those styles.
- Italic f and t are the upright forms slanted.
- The square dot now tops out at the height of l and narrows relative to the
  stem as the weight increases.

0.2.0 (2026-10-22)
- Reshaped I, a, c, e and the dot (scripts/modify.py), in every style:
  I without serifs, spaced like the stem of H; c and e with their terminals
  cut level; a with a flat top to the bowl and a tighter corner (upright
  styles); the dot of i and j square, which also squares the dot accent and
  the dieresis (uni0307 is shared) and the dots of idieresis.
  Accented composites, IJ, the mark anchors of I and Greek Iota follow.

0.1.1 (2026-10-22)
- Added ⊥ (up tack, U+22A5), drawn in the weight and width of +.

0.1.0 (2026-10-19)
- Based on Schibsted Grotesk 1.100 (commit d485f61), static instances
  Regular, Medium, Bold and their italics.
- Added Greek capitals Γ Θ Λ Ξ Φ Ψ, and the capitals shared with Latin
  (Α Β Ε Ζ Η Ι Κ Μ Ν Ο Ρ Τ Υ Χ) as references to the Latin glyphs.
- Added Greek lowercase γ δ ε ζ η θ ι κ λ ν ο ρ σ τ υ φ χ ψ ω, and the
  symbol variants ϵ (lunate epsilon) and ϕ.
- Added ∇ ∈ ∀ ∃ ∓ ⋅ ∼.
- Renamed the family to odv Grotesk.
